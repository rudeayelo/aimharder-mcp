## Context

Implementation update, 2026-10-19: the [encrypted account-boundary decision](2026-10-19-encrypted-aimharder-account-boundary.md) implements initial credential custody, verified gyms and isolated read-only account operations. Its [runbook](../hosted-aimharder-connection.md) distinguishes synthetic encryption/rotation/recovery evidence from pending platform key custody, deployment and live upstream access. Replacement/lifecycle, backup retention and restoration remain outstanding; the accepted requirements below stay current.

The portal must distinguish signing in to the service from authenticating to AimHarder and authorizing an MCP client. The existing AimHarder client uses password login and an in-memory cookie session. Session-only custody would require participants to return to the portal when a session can no longer be used; retaining recoverable credentials allows the service to authenticate again.

## Decision

- Offer Google sign-in and email/password sign-in through a maintained identity provider. Both methods may access the same portal account through an explicit linking flow that proves control of that account; linking must preserve the existing AimHarder connection and configuration. A portal password is independent of the AimHarder password. The Cloudflare/Better Auth candidate is recorded in the [runtime decision](2026-10-16-proposed-cloudflare-hosted-runtime.md); verified password admission and recovery are now covered by the #71 decisions linked above; explicit Google linking is now implemented and validated by the #72 decision and its separate synthetic, owner-reported and production read-back evidence.
- Allow one linked AimHarder account at a time per portal account. Portal and AimHarder emails need not match. Preserve access to multiple verified gyms belonging to that AimHarder account. Connecting another AimHarder account replaces the previous connection under the accepted replacement policy below; in-flight-action handling remains to be specified.
- Permit persistent encrypted storage of AimHarder credentials, recoverable by the service for upstream authentication. This is service-managed credential custody, not a claim that the service cannot decrypt the credentials. Keep credentials out of MCP inputs/results, AI conversations, logs, source control and public artifacts.
- Use `admin` for the pilot owner and `user` for other participants. Only activity publication and deletion have configurable role-based availability, initially disabled for `user` and enabled for `admin`; other existing features have no feature switches. See the [pilot decision](2026-10-15-invited-hosted-mcp-pilot.md) for the complete availability policy. Individual-user exceptions are not required.
- The administrator invites a participant by email from the portal. Admission requires proof of control of that email through Google or verified email/password sign-in. Invitations are individual, expire and can be revoked before acceptance; invited participants receive `user`. The later #71 decisions linked above specify seven-day invitations and verified Resend delivery without changing these admission requirements.
- Keep three lifecycle actions distinct: revoking one MCP client connection removes that client's access; disconnecting AimHarder removes stored upstream credentials/sessions and invalidates prepared actions; deleting the portal account additionally removes portal access and associated service data. These actions do not cancel AimHarder bookings or delete upstream activity. On account deletion, revoke access and remove active service data immediately. Earlier encrypted backups may remain for at most 30 days. Restoring a backup must preserve later deletions and revocations: it must not reactivate a deleted account, client grant or upstream connection. The user accepted this retention ceiling on 2026-10-16; platform retention and restoration enforcement must be verified.
- The administration panel shows users, roles and connection status. It does not include diagnostic messages, private training/booking data, credential viewing or participant impersonation. Omitting diagnostics from this panel does not change safe error handling for the participant or MCP client.
- The administrator may suspend a participant, blocking portal and MCP access while preserving their configuration for possible reactivation. Suspension is distinct from account deletion. Reactivation requires fresh MCP client authorization; previous grants must not silently become usable again. Any possibly sent upstream action still requires reconciliation against its original account, without retrying it.

## Account replacement

Accepted on 2026-10-16 after clarification: replacing the linked AimHarder account preserves the portal identity and existing ChatGPT/Hermes authorizations. Discard the previous upstream session and all prepared actions, verify the new AimHarder identity before using it, and require fresh preparation and confirmation for subsequent writes. A retained MCP grant must never preserve or revive the previous upstream session or action references.

MCP authorization to a portal account and the service's upstream AimHarder session are distinct. Requiring a fresh client OAuth grant was considered and rejected as unnecessary friction for this product; it is not an intrinsic protocol requirement. Changing an AimHarder password for the same verified identity is distinct from replacing that identity and likewise need not require client reauthorization. The [#81 lifecycle decision](2026-10-19-upstream-connection-lifecycle.md) now specifies queued connection commits after earlier original-account read-back, invalidation of all prior preparations and credential-free uncertainty after lost original access; an already sent write must never be retried against the new account.

## Consequences

This is a hosted-only amendment to the environment-only/no-persistence constraint in the [local credential policy](2026-09-21-credential-security-and-read-only-access.md). That constraint remains current for the local stdio package. Existing origin restrictions, identity verification, bounded authentication recovery, sanitized errors and action-specific confirmation/read-back safeguards remain required.

Persistent recoverable credentials introduce operational responsibilities: encryption-key custody and rotation, isolation, enforcement of the accepted deletion/30-day backup policy, disconnect/replacement behavior, authentication failure handling and recovery. The implementation design must provide concrete mechanisms and verification; encryption alone does not establish that these responsibilities are satisfied. Deleted credentials or grants must not be revived through backup restoration or in-flight reconciliation.

Portal-session separation amendment, 2026-10-19: an authorized offline MCP grant must survive ordinary portal sign-out. The [authorization amendment](2026-10-18-hosted-mcp-client-authorization.md) retains `offline_access` through the maintained resource policy so its existing logout hook recognizes that boundary. Browser session deletion, including a password reset that ends portal sessions, is not an implicit replacement for **Connected MCP clients**, suspension or account deletion. The new policy does not repair old issued scopes or clear an existing revocation; affected clients need fresh personal authorization. The defect and local correction are reproduced. Later fresh Hermes consent, independent new offline-family/current-grant read-back and maintained discovery/account context are observed. Later ChatGPT owner-completed reauthorization and rendered context answers are complemented by separate provider-only original-administrator/offline-family/current-grant evidence. The [ChatGPT checkpoint](../hosted-mcp-authorization.md#chatgpt-reauthorization-and-rendered-context-checkpoint-2026-10-19) preserves identity/dispatch/issuance limits; the later [Hermes trial](../hosted-mcp-authorization.md#actual-hermes-default-policy-refresh-2026-10-19) now passes maintained protected context plus same-original-grant rotation under the unchanged one-hour policy after portal sign-out, without new consent. This trial did not capture decoded JWT expiry or token HTTP/wire grant type. ChatGPT renewal remains an independent acceptance gate.

Portal login does not replace MCP authorization. Each supported client still needs an authenticated and authorized connection tied to the correct portal account. Provider and transport interoperability must be tested independently in ChatGPT and Hermes, including concurrent users and confirmed-action flows. The [runtime decision](2026-10-16-proposed-cloudflare-hosted-runtime.md) records the accepted technical candidate and outstanding compatibility gates. This lifecycle decision does not establish a working OAuth provider, database or Cloudflare runtime, or authorize a live AimHarder action.

The [MCP client authorization implementation](2026-10-18-hosted-mcp-client-authorization.md) now gives each client a durable grant tied to the verified portal identity and provides independent portal revocation with current admission checks. It preserves this decision's separate upstream-connection lifecycle and action-specific confirmation requirements. Full client and lifecycle acceptance remains pending.

Implementation update, 2026-10-19: the [activity role-access decision](2026-10-19-hosted-activity-role-access.md) implements the settled role-only policy and its same-snapshot dispatch enforcement. [Hosted publication](../hosted-activity-publication.md) retains existing exact preview/confirmation/read-back safeguards with synthetic evidence; deployment and actual client acceptance remain separate.

## Participant suspension implementation (2026-10-19)

The [suspension decision](2026-10-19-hosted-participant-suspension.md) implements minimal participant administration, atomic access/grant invalidation outside held upstream operations, fresh authorization after reactivation and preparation access generations. Configuration remains encrypted and preserved; possible sends retain original-account read-back without replay. These are synthetic checks; deletion-safe restoration and actual-client/provider acceptance remain separate.

Implementation update for #83: the [independent deletion/restoration decision](2026-10-19-independent-deletion-and-controlled-restoration.md) implements active owner deletion and an independent quarantine/manifest mechanism. It preserves the original deletion and ordinary connection-replacement requirements; stronger post-rollback fresh authentication is a distinct security recovery decision. The separate [native provider experiment](../hosted-restore-provider-validation.md) now supplies actual isolated restore/purge/reconciliation/recovery and scoped copy-closeout evidence. It does not activate the full owner runtime, prove elapsed physical expiry or establish sustainable Free capacity/real-client acceptance under #84.
